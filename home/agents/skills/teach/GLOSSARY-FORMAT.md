# Recording the workspace's language

Use `GLOSSARY.md` for concepts the user can use correctly. Introducing a term in a lesson does not qualify it for the glossary; a definition or application in the user's own explanation can.

Keep the title and a short scope statement, followed by terms:

```md
# Strength Training Glossary

Terms established while learning to plan and adjust strength training.

## Terms

**Progressive overload**:
An increase in training demand over time through load, volume, or intensity.
_Avoid_: Pushing harder, levelling up

**RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)**:
A 1–10 rating of perceived effort, where 10 is failure and 8 means two repetitions remain.
_Avoid_: Effort score, intensity rating
```

Choose one canonical name when several expressions describe the same concept; list confusing aliases under `_Avoid_`. Prefer established glossary terms in other definitions and in the rest of the course. Define each concept in one or two sentences rather than turning the entry into an exercise or procedure.

Resolve ambiguity where it matters: “In this workspace, set means a working set; warm-ups are tracked separately.” Group terms under topic headings when clusters emerge; a coherent small glossary can stay flat.

Revise definitions as understanding improves. After the user demonstrates the correction, replace the stale definition in place; preserve a non-obvious change in understanding in a learning record when it affects future lessons. The resulting glossary should express what is currently established, once per term.
